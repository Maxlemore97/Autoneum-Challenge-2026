# Presenter script — Swap, do not scrub

*One mount, three tiers - from a city shuttle to a luxury cabin*

Team 4 · Autoneum Challenge 2026 - Midterm · 2026-09-09

## The arc

Problem-solving structure with a turn. Setup: by 2035 one car serves two
owners. Problem: thirty strangers a day destroy the floor and nobody is
watching. Unfair fight: cleaning is the only thing that stops the vehicle
earning, and it takes 30 to 60 minutes. Hero: stop cleaning inside the car -
swap the contact layer, the way a hotel never cleans the mattress. The turn:
a floor you can wipe is a floor that is acoustically dead. Companion:
Autoneum solved exactly that on the outside of the car with Alpha-Liner.
Resolution: one panel, one mount, two business models - and a heated version
as the premium tier.

## Core message

> A robotaxi floor should be swapped, not scrubbed. The same mount then takes
a heated skin for the journeys people pay extra for.

## Questions to expect

The objections a sceptical audience will raise, and the answer you give. Rehearse these — a good answer to a hard question buys more credibility than a slide.

**Q — Is a heated surface not slower than blown air?**

> For a cold start, yes, and we do not claim otherwise. Radiation reaches
> the skin instantly; what takes time is the panel surface itself. That is
> why the heating layer sits directly under the wipe-clean skin and not
> under the absorber - little mass above it, so it responds in seconds
> rather than minutes.
> But the honest point is a different one. A robotaxi in service is almost
> never in a cold start. It runs sixteen hours a day, so the panel is warm
> the whole time - and the thermal mass that makes a surface slow to heat
> is exactly what makes it survive a thirty-second door opening. Air loses
> everything at every stop. The surface does not.

**Q — Is 200 W enough to heat an interior?**

> No, and it is not meant to. 200 W warms the person, not two to three
> cubic metres of air. Comfort depends on the temperature of the surfaces
> around you as much as on air temperature, which is why warm surfaces let
> you run the air about 3 C colder with one radiant source and 6 C colder
> with two, at the same comfort.
> If we claimed 200 W heats a cabin we would simply be wrong. The air
> system stays - windscreen demisting and defrosting are legal
> requirements, so it could not leave anyway. What we take away from it is
> the job of keeping people comfortable.

**Q — Why the floor and not the seat? Robotaxi interiors just look cheap today.**

> They look cheap because hard-wearing and premium are opposites today -
> anything you can hose down looks like a bus. That is the same trade-off
> as our acoustic one, on a third axis, and the wipe-clean skin breaks it:
> the premium insert is the design layer, and it clips onto the same mount.
> It should not stop at the floor. The same material language runs up the
> lower side walls and into the headliner - all Autoneum parts. We do not
> make seats, but if the surrounding surfaces set the language, the seat
> supplier matches it. Owning the surface language of a shared cabin is a
> larger position than owning one carpet.

**Q — What about premium robotaxis on longer journeys?**

> A different cell of the matrix, and it strengthens two thirds of our
> argument. The door-opening argument weakens - an intercity trip stops
> rarely. But range matters more, because a long winter trip is exactly
> where cabin heating costs you the journey. Acoustics matters far more
> over two hours than over ten minutes. And per-seat thermal zones, which
> are expensive with ducting and nearly free with surfaces, are a premium
> feature.
> Content per vehicle is also higher there: more trim, more acoustic
> material, a headliner that matters. We built the pitch on the city case
> because that is where the cleaning problem bites hardest - but the panel
> travels into the premium segment without changing.

### 8 — A floor you can wipe is a floor that is acoustically dead

**Beat:** The turn · **Layout:** `statement`

**On screen**

- kicker: The harder problem
- body: Absorption needs an open, porous surface. Cleaning wants a closed one. Today you pick one.

**Say**

> Here is where the idea nearly died. An acoustic floor has to be open and
> porous so sound can get in and be absorbed. A cleanable floor wants to be
> closed so nothing gets in. Today you choose - a carpet that absorbs and
> stains, or a rubber mat that wipes and is loud. In a battery-electric
> robotaxi without engine noise that is worse, because road and wind noise
> are all that is left.
