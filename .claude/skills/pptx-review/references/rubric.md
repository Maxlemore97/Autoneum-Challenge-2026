# What the deck is marked against

Two different rubrics, two different weeks. Using the wrong one is the most
expensive mistake in this module.

## The grade

| | Teilaufgabe I | Teilaufgabe II | Teilaufgabe III |
|---|---|---|---|
| | Midterm Presentation | **Final Presentation** | Blog-Fachartikel |
| Date | Wed 09 Sep | Fri 11 Sep | 30 Sep |
| Share of the module grade | 15 % | **70 %** | 15 % |

Marked in tenths of a grade. The group grade may be redistributed within the
group.

## Midterm — «Idee & fachliche Basis» (15 %)

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| **Idee** | 50 % |
| **Recherche** | 30 % |
| Foliendesign-Konzept | 20 % |

Substance, not polish. A rough deck with a strong, well-evidenced idea beats a
beautiful deck with a thin one. Design is only asked for as a *concept* here.

## Final — «Storytelling & Kommunikation» (70 %)

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| **Story** | 50 % |
| Foliendesign | 30 % |
| Auftritt | 20 % |

The idea is no longer marked. Half the mark — of the 70 % half of the module —
is the story. A deck that is factually excellent and narratively flat fails
here, and that is the trap this rubric is built to catch.

Framing: the final is a **Vorstandspräsentation**. Board members decide; they
do not study.

## Autoneum's rules of the game — hard gates

Failing one of these is not a lost point, it is off-brief:

- Presentations must be **in English**
- **State clearly the use case and vehicle segment**
- **Put yourself in 2035**, assume full autonomous driving works
- **Next-step innovation** — not what already exists, not science fiction;
  **list your sources** when available
- Get inspired by other industries and markets
- Consider your generation, but not exclusively
- Regional (European & Swiss) focus is fine
- Think holistically about how users and goods interact with their
  surroundings, ensuring **safety and sustainability**
- **Put yourselves in the shoes of a supplier, not a carmaker**
- Be creative — **do not limit yourselves to just PowerPoint**

That last one is scored under *Auftritt* and *Story* in practice. A deck with no
artefact, demonstration or measurement behind it has left points on the table.

## What the lecture says a good deck is

Used as the standard for *Story* and *Foliendesign*. Full detail in the
`pptx-deck` skill's `references/design-rules.md`.

- Botschaft → Story → Visualisierung, in that order
- Pyramid principle: conclusion first, support underneath
- Action titles that read as a screenplay
- The audience wants to be impressed before informed
- Picture before text; 8-second attention span
- Focus rather than completeness; white space is a design element
- Consistency and standardisation across slides
- Fokussieren · Reduzieren · Emotionalisieren

## Scoring

Score each criterion 1–6 in tenths (Swiss scale, 4.0 passes). Weight, sum,
report. Be the sceptical examiner, not the supportive teammate — **an inflated
score is a failure of this skill**. A 6 means it would survive a board that is
looking for the hole. Where evidence is thin, score lower and name what would
move it.
